Introduction & Executive Summary

Lesson 2 Overview

Analysis of an Expansion Project

Evaluating new product lines, facilities, or market entries requires strict cash flow forecasting and comprehensive risk adjustment.

1. Cash Flow Identification

Collect data on incremental cash flows. Account for depreciation tax shields, cannibalization side effects, opportunity costs, and ignore sunk costs completely.

2. Risk Categorization

Distinguish between **Stand-Alone Risk**, **Corporate Risk**, and **Market (Beta) Risk** to adjust required hurdle rates accurately.

3. Portfolio Diversification

Recognize how firm-level diversification reduces corporate risk, while well-diversified shareholders focus exclusively on market beta.

4. Decision Framework

Incorporate sensitivity analysis and hurdle rate adjustments to make sound capital allocation decisions under uncertainty.

Golden Rule of Expansion: Focus solely on incremental, after-tax cash flows. Past expenditures are irrelevant!

Project Risk Dimensions

3 Types of Risk in Capital Budgeting

ISO VIEW

Stand-Alone Risk

Project in Isolation

Assumes (a) the project is the firm's only asset and (b) the firm is the only stock in the investor's portfolio.

Measured By: Standard Dev (σ)
Diversification: Totally Ignored
FIRM VIEW

Corporate Risk

Within-Firm Asset Portfolio

Considers that the project is one of many assets owned by the corporation. Some risk is eliminated by internal product line diversification.

Measured By: Impact on Firm Returns
Diversification: Within Corporation
MARKET VIEW

Market (Beta) Risk

Well-Diversified Shareholder

Risk from the perspective of a stockholder holding a broad stock portfolio. Focuses on non-diversifiable systematic volatility.

Measured By: Project Beta (β)
Diversification: Full Market Portfolio
Summary & Application

Strategic Capital Budgeting Decision Rules

Incremental Cash Flow Guidelines

  • Include Opportunity Costs: Add foregone revenue from existing assets to project outlays.
  • Deduct Side Effects: Account for cannibalization of existing product line sales.
  • Ignore Sunk Costs: Past feasibility studies and historical R&D must never alter NPV decisions.

Risk Adjustment Rules

Most projects use Market (Beta) Risk as the primary benchmark because stock price is determined in well-diversified markets.

High Risk Project: WACC + 2% to 5%
Average Risk Expansion: Base WACC
Low Risk Replacement: WACC - 2%

Tax Shield Mechanics

Depreciation Tax Shield Effect

Although depreciation is a non-cash expense, it reduces taxable operating income. The annual tax savings generated is equal to:

Tax Shield Inflow = Depreciation Expense (D) × Marginal Tax Rate (T)

• Straight-Line Depreciation: Equal annual tax shield throughout life.

• Accelerated Depreciation (MACRS): High tax shields in early years increase Net Present Value (NPV) due to the time value of money.

Revenue Erosion

Cannibalization & Side Effects

When a company introduces a new product line (e.g., launching an SUV model), it may decrease sales of its existing sedans. This lost gross profit is a **cannibalization cost** and must be subtracted from the new project's cash flow forecast.

Net Incremental Revenue = New Project Sales - Lost Existing Sales

Foregone Alternatives

Opportunity Costs

If an expansion project utilizes land or buildings already owned by the firm, the market value or rental income foregone by not selling/leasing that asset **MUST** be charged as an initial cash outflow to the project.

Total Initial Cash Outflow = Equipment Cost + Land Opportunity Cost (After-Tax)

Example Scenario:

A firm owns a vacant warehouse that could be sold today for $500,000 (after taxes) or converted into an expansion plant.

Correct Capital Budgeting Treatment:

Charge +$500,000 to Year 0 Cash Outflows!

CRITICAL ACCOUNTING RULE

Sunk Costs MUST BE EXCLUDED!

A sunk cost is an outlay that has already occurred or has been committed to regardless of whether the project is accepted or rejected. Examples include $100,000 spent last year on a market feasibility study or preliminary engineering design.

Why Ignore Sunk Costs?

Capital budgeting evaluates future incremental cash flows. Because sunk costs cannot be changed by the current decision, including them distorts the true NPV.

Mental Check Question:

"If I drop this project right now, will I get this money back?" If answer is NO → Sunk Cost → EXCLUDE!
